# Purchases
> Status: LEGACY DIRECT PURCHASE + PROCURE-TO-PAY THROUGH PUR-150 IMPLEMENTED | Last reviewed: 2026-10-11

The implemented workflow described below is a direct supplier invoice and inventory receipt, not a Purchase Order lifecycle. The approved additive evolution, compatibility rules and ordered delivery slices are documented in `docs/PURCHASE_MANAGEMENT_EVOLUTION_PLAN.md`. Existing Purchase and PurchaseReturn history will remain valid and will not be fabricated into new procurement documents.

PUR-005 context ownership and accounting gates are accepted in `docs/PURCHASE_ARCHITECTURE_ALIGNMENT.md`: Supplier remains the current counterparty, Purchasing owns requests/sourcing/orders, Inventory owns receipt stock effects, and Accounting/AP owns bills, payables and payments. These are target boundaries, not implemented new documents.

PUR-010 adds tenant-safe supplier/product purchasing information with purchase unit, supplier code/description, minimum quantity, order multiple, base-currency price, validity, lead time, priority, preferred/active state and rowversion. It excludes prepared-to-order products and enforces purchase-unit compatibility with the product stock unit. The dedicated bilingual screen uses separate view/manage permissions and has no stock or GL side effect. Migration `AddSupplierPurchasingData` is applied to `AHMAD/MiniStoreDb`.

The additive procurement workflow now also supports internal Purchase Requests, configurable approvals, sourcing invitations, submitted supplier quotations, a reasoned quotation award and a new Purchase Order. A Purchase Order is created only from that award, freezes its supplier, warehouse, currency, dates, payment terms and commercial line data, and moves through Draft -> Approved -> Confirmed (or a reasoned cancellation before confirmation). The list/detail UI respects distinct view/create/approve/confirm/cancel permissions. A confirmed order remains a commercial commitment: it does not change inventory, AVCO, stock movements or accounting. Receipt/billing-derived completion is intentionally deferred to PUR-060 onward.

PUR-060 adds an additive Goods Receipt (GRN) flow from a confirmed Purchase Order. A user records only physically received quantities; each line is limited to the remaining ordered quantity across previously posted receipts. The Application service creates and posts the GRN inside one Serializable UnitOfWork, then delegates ProductStock/AVCO, tracked lot/serial receipt and immutable StockTransaction creation to Inventory-owned services and entities. It also writes one idempotent `ReceiptIn` physical StockMovement per persisted receipt line. A disposable SQL concurrency fixture proves that two full-quantity attempts yield exactly one receipt and one set of inventory effects. PUR-065A values new receipts at the frozen PO unit price after line discount and, when the receipt has positive value, posts Inventory/GRNI through the central journal gateway in that same transaction. Controlled reversal and inventory-to-GL reconciliation reporting remain open.

PUR-070 adds immutable returns against posted new-flow Goods Receipts without changing legacy Purchase Returns. Each line is capped by its received quantity minus prior posted returns and posted Vendor Bill allocations, converts back to base stock quantity, validates current availability and reuses location/lot/serial removal controls. Posting atomically creates the return document, StockTransaction, `ReceiptReturnOut` physical movement and journal: debit GRNI at original receipt value, credit Inventory at removed AVCO cost and send any difference to the configured Purchase Price Variance account. Already billed quantities require a future supplier-credit-note flow and cannot be returned through this unbilled reversal path.

PUR-080 adds separate Vendor Bills against confirmed Purchase Orders and their posted receipt lines. One bill may allocate a partial remaining quantity from multiple receipts for the order; cumulative posted bills and posted returns reduce availability. Supplier invoice numbers are normalized to letters/digits and protected by a tenant/supplier filtered unique index. Posting freezes the entered price and tax policy/account, debits GRNI at receipt value, sends the bill-net difference to Purchase Price Variance, debits recoverable input tax and credits the supplier's configured payable account in the same Serializable transaction. The SQL fixture proves duplicate concurrent supplier invoices yield exactly one bill and one balanced journal.

PUR-090 gates Vendor Bill posting with a three-way comparison. Tenant settings define quantity and net-price tolerance percentages. Receipt allocation remains the quantity authority; an over-allocation clears only the available receipt quantity and becomes an exception/variance. Price matching compares tax-exclusive bill unit price to the frozen receipt/PO net unit cost. Differences above tolerance block normal posting; `VendorBills.OverrideMatch` may post only with a reason. Every posted bill receives one immutable match run, applied tolerance snapshots and line exceptions, and the override actor/reason/time when used.

PUR-100 adds Supplier Payments against posted Vendor Bills. One payment may partially allocate several open bills for the same supplier and currency. Cumulative allocations are recalculated inside a Serializable transaction and cannot exceed any bill balance. Posting debits the supplier's payable account and credits the selected active payment method's cash/bank account through the central journal gateway; payment number, allocations and journal commit atomically.

PUR-110 bridges reviewed replenishment to purchasing without creating an order directly. A purchase-sourced rule below its minimum can explicitly create one Draft Purchase Request for its recalculated target quantity. Valid preferred supplier terms select the purchase unit and supplier, round up to minimum/order multiples and extend the needed date by the longer lead time. Source provenance plus a filtered tenant-unique index prevents concurrent duplicate active requests; the resulting draft still follows normal submission, approval, sourcing, quotation and order steps.

PUR-120 adds Purchase Agreements only from a recorded winning quotation. The agreement freezes supplier, warehouse, currency, payment terms and awarded line pricing while defining a maximum contracted quantity and validity window. Activation and cancellation are rowversion-protected; each release recalculates non-cancelled cumulative quantities inside the Serializable UnitOfWork and creates a Draft Purchase Order linked to its agreement and agreement lines. The released order still requires the normal approve/confirm flow and has no inventory or accounting effect until receipt. A direct order and an agreement cannot both be created from the same award.

PUR-120 also adds repeat Purchase Templates saved from an existing Purchase Order. A template freezes product, managed unit, requested quantity, supplier, warehouse, priority and lead days. Every generation receives a monotonic source reference and creates a Draft Purchase Request, so submission, approvals, sourcing and quotation selection remain mandatory. Inactive templates reject generation, and current product/unit eligibility is revalidated before the draft is created.

PUR-130 adds immutable, centrally numbered Landed Cost documents against posted Goods Receipts. Freight, customs, insurance and other positive components are allocated automatically by base-stock quantity or frozen receipt net value. For each product, the still-on-hand share is capped by current warehouse stock relative to the receipt's product quantity and is added to AVCO through a zero-quantity value movement; the consumed share is posted to Cost of Sales. One atomic journal debits Inventory and/or Cost of Sales and credits the configured Landed Cost Clearing account. Normalized external references are tenant-unique, allocation/capitalization snapshots remain auditable, and the flow assumes company base currency until an exchange-rate policy exists.

PUR-140 adds a read-only purchasing analytics screen with a bounded date filter, operational cards and supplier performance rows. Open and overdue counts are current confirmed orders with unreceived quantity. Receipt value and landed cost use their posting dates; billed spend, match exceptions, overrides and price variance use bill dates; outstanding payables are current posted bill balances. Supplier fulfilment, on-time delivery, average delivery days and returns use the approved/confirmed/closed order cohort dated inside the selected period together with all posted receipt and return evidence for those orders. Rates safely return zero without a denominator and cap at 100 percent. The projection reads existing tenant-filtered repositories, creates no reporting tables and has no inventory or accounting side effect.

PUR-150 adds barcode-assisted receiving against confirmed Purchase Orders. The operator creates an empty Draft Goods Receipt, scans an exact tenant product barcode or code, supplies quantity and any required lot/serial/date evidence, reviews the accumulated order lines, then explicitly posts through the existing Goods Receipt posting path. Each scan has a strongly scoped, tenant-unique retry key and immutable audit row; repeating the same receipt/token/quantity request is a no-op, while reusing its key for a different receipt, product token or quantity is rejected. Scans cannot exceed the open ordered quantity or silently choose between duplicate order lines. Draft scans have no stock, tracking, movement or accounting effect. Final posting revalidates the order and quantities and atomically applies the established inventory, tracking, AVCO, ReceiptIn and Inventory/GRNI effects.

Creates a purchase header/items after validating supplier, each line's warehouse, active product, quantity and non-negative price. Prepared-to-order products are excluded because their ingredients, not the menu item, are purchased. One invoice can distribute products across warehouses. Exact rack/bin is not requested, so receipts enter Unassigned Stock. Lot-tracked lines require a lot number and may capture manufacture/expiration dates; serial-tracked lines require one unique serial per received unit. The tracked balance and immutable receipt trace are committed with the purchase. A line total is quantity × price minus fixed line discount; its net unit cost updates the target product/warehouse moving average inside the same UnitOfWork and the purchase movement freezes the full valuation change. Recoverable input tax is excluded from inventory cost, including tax extracted from price-inclusive lines.

From purchase details, a permitted user can post once to the ledger. Posting uses each line's warehouse inventory account and dimension, plus input tax, purchase discount and supplier payable accounts. Receipt movements with provisional-negative cost variance add a COGS/inventory adjustment in the correct debit/credit direction for each affected warehouse. The supplier invoice number remains an external reference; the generated journal entry receives its own centralized JournalEntry sequence number in the posting transaction. Historical items inherit their header warehouse.

Posted purchases support immutable partial/full supplier returns. Cumulative returned quantity cannot exceed the purchase line and current stock must cover the return. Tracked returns require explicit lot quantities or unique serial identifiers received by the original purchase; the selected identity must still be available in that warehouse. Stock leaves at current moving-average cost. Supplier payable, input tax and purchase discount reverse proportionally from the original invoice, with the original-versus-current inventory difference posted to COGS as return cost variance. The return and stock movement use a separate PRT sequence and the accounting entry is created atomically through the central gateway, so Open fiscal-period rules apply. Sources: purchase/return entities and DTOs, `PurchaseService`, `PurchasePostingService`, `PurchaseReturnService`, repositories/configurations and purchase/return screens. General cancellation remains unimplemented.
